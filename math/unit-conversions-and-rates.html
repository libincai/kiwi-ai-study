Unit Conversions and Rates

This section explores the mathematical relationship between rates and physical quantities. By treating units as algebraic variables, we can reliably transform measurements across different scales using the principle of dimensional analysis.

Advanced Version: Dimensional Analysis

Example with speed conversion

Convert 90 km/h → m/s.

∵ 1km=1000m1km = 1000m, and 1h=3,600s1h = 3,600s

Set up the chain:

90kmh×1000m1km×1h3600s=90×10003600ms=25m/s\frac{90\,\boxed{\text{km}}}{\boxed{\text{h}}} \times \frac{1000\,\text{m}}{1\,\boxed{\text{km}}} \times \frac{1\,\boxed{\text{h}}}{3600\,\text{s}} = \frac{90 \times 1000}{3600}\,\frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = 25\, m/s

∴ 90 km/h = 25 m/s

Example with density

Convert 1 g/cm3 → kg/L.

∵ 1kg=1000g1kg = 1000g, and 1L=1,000cm31L = 1,000cm3

Set up the chain:

1gcm3×1kg1000g×1000cm31L=10001000kgL=1kgL\frac{1\,\boxed{\text{g}}}{\boxed{\text{cm}}^{3}} \times \frac{1\,\text{kg}}{1000\,\boxed{\text{g}}} \times \frac{1000\,\boxed{\text{cm}}^{3}}{1\,\text{L}} = \frac{1000}{1000}\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{L}} = 1\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{L}}

∴ 1 g/cm3 = 1 kg/L
