Trigonometric Identities

What Is an Identity?

A trigonometric identity is an equation that is true for every angle for which both sides are defined. Identities can be used to simplify expressions and prove two forms are equivalent.

Pythagorean Identity

This follows from the unit circle and the Pythagorean theorem on the right triangle formed by x, y, and r = 1.

Quotient Identities

These hold wherever the denominators are nonzero.

Strategies for Proving Trigonometric Identities

Begin with the more complicated side. Convert sec, csc, cot, and tan to sine and cosine when useful. Look for common denominators, factoring, expansion, and the Pythagorean identity. Work on one side until it matches the other; do not assume the identity is already true.

Useful algebra: factor expressions, combine fractions, multiply numerator and denominator by the same nonzero expression, and use conjugates where appropriate. State domain restrictions if a denominator could be zero.

Quick Checks
  1. Simplify cos(135°) × tan(135°).
  2. Show that tan θ × cos θ = sin θ whenever defined.
  3. Explain why csc θ is undefined when sin θ = 0.
