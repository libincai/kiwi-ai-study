Chance and Probability

This covers the mathematics of likelihood and randomness.

Outcome

An outcome is a single possible result of an experiment or action.

Examples:

  • When rolling a standard die, getting a ‘3’ is an outcome.

  • When flipping a coin, getting ‘Heads’ is an outcome.

Event

An event is one specific result or a collection of specific results that you are interested in. It is a subset of the sample space.

Examples:

  • The event “rolling an even number” on a die includes the outcomes {2,4,6}.

  • The event “drawing a red card” from a deck includes all hearts and diamonds.

Sample Space

The sample space is the complete set of all possible outcomes for a given experiment or situation.

Examples:

  • The sample space for rolling a standard die is {1,2,3,4,5,6}.

  • The sample space for flipping a coin is {Heads,Tails}.

Probability

Probability is a measure of how likely an event is to occur. It is expressed as a number between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain), or as a percentage between 0% and 100%.

Example:

The probability of rolling a ‘4’ on a die is 16 or approximately 16.7%.

Theoretical Probability

Theoretical probability (math-based) is the likelihood of an event happening based on mathematical reasoning and ideal conditions, assuming all outcomes are equally likely. It is what should happen.

P(Event)=Number of favorable outcomesTotal number of outcomes in sample spaceP\left( \text{Event} \right) = \frac{\text{Number~of~favorable~outcomes}}{\text{Total~number~of~outcomes~in~sample~space}}

Example:

The theoretical probability of flipping a coin and getting heads is 12 (one favorable outcome out of two total outcomes).

Experimental Probability

Experimental probability (data-based) is the likelihood of an event happening based on actual data collected from performing an experiment or test. It is what actually happens in practice.

P(Event)=Number of times the event occurredTotal number of trials in the experimentP\left( \text{Event} \right) = \frac{\text{Number~of~times~the~event~occurred}}{\text{Total~number~of~trials~in~the~experiment}}

Example:

If you flip a coin 100 times and get heads 48 times, the experimental probability of heads is 48,100 or 48%.

Certain, Likely, Unlikely, Impossible

These are words used to describe probability using a scale of likelihood:

  • Impossible (Probability = 0): The event will never happen (e.g., rolling a 7 on a 6-sided die).

  • Unlikely (Probability < 0.5): The event has a small chance of happening (e.g., winning the lottery).

  • Likely (Probability > 0.5): The event will probably happen (e.g., the sun rising tomorrow).

  • Certain (Probability = 1): The event will definitely happen (e.g., rolling a number less than 7 on a 6-sided die).

Independent and Dependent Events

Independent events

Events where the outcome of the first event does not affect the outcome of the second event.

Examples:

Rolling a die and then flipping a coin.

Dependent events

Events where the outcome of the first event does affect the outcome of the second event.

Example:

Drawing two cards from a deck without replacement (the second draw depends on what was drawn first).
