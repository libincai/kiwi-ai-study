Six Trigonometric Functions on the Unit Circle

A unit circle is centered at O and has radius R = 1. Let the point on the circle at angle θ have coordinates (x, y). For the first-quadrant angle shown, each trigonometric function can be represented by a length.

Sine — sin θ

Sine = opposite ÷ hypotenuse. Since the hypotenuse is the unit radius, sin θ = y/R = y/1 = y. Thus sin θ is the y-coordinate.

Cosine — cos θ

Cosine = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse. Since R = 1, cos θ = x/R = x/1 = x. Thus cos θ is the x-coordinate.

Tangent — tan θ

In right triangle OAB, OA = 1. Therefore tan θ = AB/OA = AB/1 = AB.

Cotangent — cot θ

FC is parallel to the positive x-axis, so the angle at C equals θ (corresponding angles). In right triangle OFC, OF = 1. Therefore cot θ = FC/OF = FC/1 = FC.

Secant — sec θ

DE is tangent to the circle at the point on the terminal arm, so it is perpendicular to the radius. In the right triangle with hypotenuse OE, cos θ = R/OE = 1/OE. Therefore sec θ = 1/cos θ = OE.

Cosecant — csc θ

Using the same tangent and radius, the right triangle with hypotenuse OD gives sin θ = R/OD = 1/OD. Therefore csc θ = 1/sin θ = OD.

Summary: sin θ = y; cos θ = x; tan θ = AB; cot θ = FC; sec θ = OE; csc θ = OD.

Note: These positive segment-length interpretations apply to the first quadrant shown. In other quadrants, trigonometric signs must also be considered.

The ratios are undefined when their denominators are zero. Do not confuse reciprocal ratios with inverse trigonometric functions (arcsin, arccos, arctan).
