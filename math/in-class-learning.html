In-Class Learning

Guided mathematics learning that builds understanding and independent thinking.

Why guided learning matters

Free resources: learn at your own pace

Students can read, review, and revisit explanations whenever they need. This is a valuable way to build familiarity and independence.

In class: learn through interaction

Students can ask questions at the moment they get stuck, receive feedback, compare approaches, explain their reasoning, and practise with guidance.

Our teaching philosophy

Understand why, not just how

Explore where a formula comes from and why a rule works, rather than memorizing steps without meaning.

Think before memorizing

Use reasoning, observation, and discovery to build knowledge that can transfer to unfamiliar problems.

Connect numbers, shapes, and ideas

Use algebra, geometry, diagrams, graphs, and real-world examples to make connections visible.

Explain to understand

Students are encouraged to describe a method in their own words and teach back what they have learned.

A monthly learning cycle

A typical four-week cycle can combine guided teaching, independent practice, review, and student explanation. The exact pace and content are adapted to the learner and course.

1

Learn & Understand

Introduce concepts, explain reasoning, and work through examples together.

2

Practise & Connect

Build fluency through guided practice and connect new ideas to prior knowledge.

3

Apply & Strengthen

Solve varied problems, revisit challenging points, and strengthen independent thinking.

4

Teach Back & Reflect

Students explain concepts and methods in their own words, revealing what they truly understand.

The four-week cycle describes a teaching approach, not a fixed promise that every learner or course follows an identical schedule.

Learning by grade and level

Middle School · Grades 6–8

Strengthen number sense, operations, fractions, ratios, algebra foundations, geometry, and mathematical confidence.

High School · Grades 9–12

Develop deeper understanding of algebra, functions, quadratic relations, analytic geometry, trigonometry, and problem solving.

Mathematical Enrichment

Explore creative strategies, formula derivations, geometry theorems, number patterns, and ideas beyond routine exercises.

Individual learning needs

Learning goals, pace, and practice can be adjusted after understanding a student's current level and needs.

Meet your instructor

Libin Cai

I have loved mathematics since childhood. It has always been one of my strongest and favourite subjects. In the year I took China's national university entrance examination, the mathematics paper was particularly challenging, yet I achieved a result very close to a perfect score. My university mathematics results were also consistently strong.

From the time my child began exploring early mathematical ideas, I have been closely involved in supporting and guiding their learning. This ongoing experience has given me a thorough understanding of Ontario mathematics, from Grade 1 through high school, including its curriculum expectations and the progression of concepts across grades.

I enjoy exploring the patterns and connections behind mathematics. I am especially drawn to visual mathematics, using everyday examples to make abstract ideas easier to understand, and discovering patterns through observation and reasoning. Rather than simply memorizing existing formulas, I enjoy working from mathematical principles to derive formulas and uncover new strategies.

In my teaching, I aim to make complex ideas intuitive and accessible. For example, when my child struggled with 90-degree rotations of geometric figures, I introduced the right-hand rule to turn an abstract coordinate transformation into a simple, visual method. This helped make the direction and pattern of rotation much easier to understand.

I value the quality of practice more than the quantity of questions. Instead of assigning large numbers of repetitive exercises, I prefer a smaller number of carefully chosen, thought-provoking problems that help students understand concepts, recognize patterns, and explore different approaches. Practice is important for building accuracy and fluency, but it should deepen understanding rather than become mechanical repetition.

My goal is not simply to teach students how to solve problems, but to develop their mathematical understanding, logical reasoning, and independent thinking—so they can see the beauty of mathematics, understand why it works, and grow in confidence.
