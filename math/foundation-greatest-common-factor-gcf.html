Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The Greatest Common Factor (GCF) of two or more whole numbers is the largest number that divides all of them without leaving a remainder.

Examples:

  • GCF(8,12)=4GCF(8,12) = 4

  • GCF(18,24)=6GCF(18,24) = 6

  • GCF(20,50)=10GCF(20,50) = 10

In higher mathematics, the same idea is called the Greatest Common Divisor (GCD). Both mean the exact same thing.

In the examples below, you will see GCD inside math formula, even though we use GCF in the text for learning.

Basic Skills

There are three simple and reliable beginner-friendly ways:

List all factors

List all factors of each number and choose the largest one they share.

Example:

Find GCF (15, 25).

  • 15 → 1, 3, 5, 15

  • 25 → 1, 5, 25

GCF = 5

Prime factorization

Break each number into its prime factors and multiply the primes they share.

Examples:

Find GCF (18, 24).

  • 18=2×3×318 = 2 \times 3 \times 3

  • 24=2×2×2×324 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3

∵ Shared primes → 2 and 3
∴ GCF =2×3=6

Start from the smaller number’s factors

List the factors of the smaller number and check them from largest to smallest.

Examples:

Find GCF (20, 32).

Factors of 20 → 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20

Check from biggest:

  • 20? no

  • 10? no

  • 5? no

  • 4? yes

∴ GCF = 4
