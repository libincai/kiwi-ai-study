Word Problems

A Structured Approach to Solving Word Problems

Word problems vary in complexity. Some require only a few calculations, while others involve multiple conditions, several steps, or relationships that are not immediately obvious.

To solve them effectively, use the following four-step framework.

1. Given

Identify and organize the important information provided in the problem. Record the known quantities, conditions, and relationships concisely using words, numbers, symbols, or diagrams.

2. Problem

Clearly state what you need to find. Identify the unknown quantities and define variables when necessary.

3. Solution

Choose an appropriate strategy, such as drawing a diagram, making a table, identifying a pattern, or forming an equation. Then carry out the calculations step by step, explaining your reasoning clearly.

Use because ∵\because to explain why a mathematical statement or step is valid. Use therefore ∴\therefore to introduce a conclusion that follows from your reasoning.

4. Verification

Check your answer against the original conditions. Confirm that the result is reasonable, uses the correct units, and answers the question.

Use because ∵\because to explain why your answer satisfies the given conditions, and therefore ∴\therefore to state your conclusion.

When Should You Use This Framework?

  • Moderate to complex word problems: Use all four steps — Given, Problem, Solution, and Verification — to organize your thinking and communicate your reasoning clearly.

  • Simple word problems: You may go directly to Solution and Verification without writing separate Given and Problem sections.

Important: Solution and Verification are mandatory for every word problem. Always show sufficient working to explain how you obtained your answer, and check that your final answer satisfies the original conditions.

Example

Because the length is 3 cm greater than the width, the length is x+3x + 3 when the width is xx.

Because the area is 40 cm240\text{~cm}^{2}, we can form the equation xx+3=40x\left. x + 3 \right.\ = 40.

Solving the equation gives x=5x = 5 or x=−8x = - 8. Since a width must be positive, x=5x = 5.

Therefore, the width is 5 cm and the length is 8 cm.

Verification:

8−5=38×5=40 cm2{8 - 5 = 3 }{8 \times 5 = 40\text{~cm}^{2}}

Both original conditions are satisfied. Therefore, the answer is correct.

As you gain experience, you will learn to recognize which problems need a detailed plan, and which can be solved directly. The goal is not to write more, but to think clearly, solve efficiently, and verify confidently.
