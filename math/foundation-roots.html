Roots

A root asks: “What number was raised to this power to get this number?”

We can write any root like this:

  • 4\sqrt{4} → square root of 4
  • 83\sqrt[3]{8} → cube root of 8
  • 164\sqrt[4]{16} → 4th root of 16
  • xn\sqrt[n]{x} → n-th root of x
Square Root

A square root of a number 𝑛 is a number 𝑟 such that 𝑟2 = 𝑛.

Example:

4\sqrt{4} → the square root of 4, also said as:

  • the 2nd root of 4
  • the second root of 4
  • root 2 of 4

All mean: “what number squared gives 4?” → answer is 2.

Cube Root

A cube root of a number 𝑛 is a number 𝑟 such that r3=nr^{3} = n.

Example:

83\sqrt[3]{8} → the cube root of 8, also said as:

  • the 3rd root of 8
  • root 3 of 8

→ answer is 2, because 23=8.

Higher Roots

A higher root tells what number must be multiplied by itself several times to get a given number.

The small number written above the radical sign (\sqrt{\vphantom{1}}) is called the index.

Examples:

  • 164\sqrt[4]{16} → “the 4th root of 16” or “root 4 of 16”
  • xn\sqrt[n]{x} → the n-th root of x
Reverse Operation Pair

Just like + and − are opposites, and × and ÷ are opposites, exponent and root are also a reverse operation pair — they undo each other.

  • squared ↔︎ square root (𝑥2 ↔︎ x\sqrt{x})
  • cubed ↔︎ cube root (𝑥3 ↔︎ x3\sqrt[3]{x})
  • x to the n-th power ↔︎ n-th root of x (𝑥𝑛 ↔︎ xn\sqrt[n]{x})
Even Roots: the ± Pair and the \sqrt{\vphantom{1}} Sign

Two numbers can have the same even power. They are opposites of each other and are both roots of the equation.

Example:

x2=4→x=±2x^{2} = 4 \rightarrow x\ = \pm \ 2

Solving an equation: the square roots of 4 are +2 and −2.

Using the \sqrt{\vphantom{1}} symbol: 4=+2\sqrt{4} = +2 only. The radical symbol \sqrt{\vphantom{1}} represents the principal (nonnegative) square root.

Remember: Solving an equation gives both roots; the \sqrt{\vphantom{1}} symbol gives only the principal square root.

Even root: ± values; \sqrt{\vphantom{1}}: + only.
Solving an Equation
x² = 4 Two solutions x = ±2
x² = 9 Two solutions x = ±3
Using the \sqrt{\vphantom{1}} Symbol
4\sqrt{4} Principal (nonnegative) square root +2
±4\pm\sqrt{4} Both roots ±2
−4\sqrt{-4} Negative under even root Not a real #
9\sqrt{9} Principal (nonnegative) square root +3
Odd Roots: One Real Value

An odd-index root has exactly one real value. The radicand (the number under the radical) can be positive, negative, or zero.

Examples:

83=2\sqrt[3]{8} = 2

−83=−2\sqrt[3]{-8} = -2

Odd root → one real value. No ± is needed.
Positive Radicands
83\sqrt[3]{8} Positive number under an odd root 2
273\sqrt[3]{27} Positive number under an odd root 3
643\sqrt[3]{64} Positive number under an odd root 4
Negative Radicands
−83\sqrt[3]{-8} Negative number under an odd root −2
−273\sqrt[3]{-27} Negative number under an odd root −3
−643\sqrt[3]{-64} Negative number under an odd root −4

Remember: Odd roots can have positive or negative radicands, but each has only one real value. No ± is needed.
