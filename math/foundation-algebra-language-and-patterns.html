Algebra Language and Patterns

Term and Factor

Term

A term is one part of an expression or equation, separated by + or −.

Example:

2x+5=92x + 5 = 9

Here, 2𝑥 and 5 are terms.

💡Learning Insight
× and ÷ do not separate terms — they stay inside the term.

Factor

Inside a term, the numbers and letters joined by multiplication or division are called factors.

Example:

4xy−92+354xy - 9^{2} + \frac{3}{5}

  • In 4xy4xy, the factors are 44, xx, and yy.

  • In 929^{2}, the factor is 99, multiplied by itself.

  • In 35\frac{3}{5} , factors are 3 and 5.

NOTE: If you intended to write 4xy−9×24xy - 9 \times 2\ rather than 4xy−924xy - 9^{2}, then the factors in 9×29 \times 2\ are 99 and 22. The superscript makes a difference!

💡Learning Insight
Plus and minus separate terms; multiplication and division connect factors.

Variable and Coefficient

Variable

Variable is a letter that stands for a number we don’t know yet.

Example:

  • 2x+5=92x + 5 = 9

In this equation, 𝑥 is the variable.

Coefficient

Coefficient is the number in front of a variable.

Examples:

  • in 5𝑥, the coefficient is 5

  • in −3𝑦, the coefficient is −3

  • in 12𝑥, the coefficient is 1/2

  • in 𝑥 (no number shown), the coefficient is 1 (because 𝑥 really means 1𝑥)

Like Terms

Like terms are terms that have the exact same variables and the exact same exponents for those variables. Only the coefficients can be different.

Examples:

  • 2𝑥 and 7𝑥 are like terms.

  • 5y2y^{2} and y2y^{2} are like terms.

  • 3𝑎𝑏 and −2𝑎𝑏 are like terms.

  • 4𝑥 and 4x2x^{2} are not like terms (different exponents).

  • 𝑎 and 𝑏 are not like terms (different variables).

Constant

A constant is a fixed value that does not change in a problem. In an expression, any number without a variable is a constant term.

Examples:

  • 2x+52x + 5→ the constant term is 5

  • y−7=12y - 7 = 12→ the constants are −7 and 12

  • 3a+4b−93a + 4b - 9→ the constant term is −9

  • −0.5x2+6- 0.5x^{2} + 6→ the constant term is 6

Expression, Equation and Inequality

Expression

Expression is a math phrase made with numbers or variables but without an equal sign.

Examples:

  • 2x+572x + 57

  • y−3y - 3

Equation

Equation is a math sentence that shows two sides are equal. An equation always includes an equal sign.

Examples:

  • 3+4=73 + 4 = 7

  • 2x+5=72x + 5 = 7

Inequality

Inequality is a math sentence that shows two sides are not equal, using inequality symbols.

Examples:

  • x>5x > 5 (xx is greater than 5)

  • y≤10y \leq 10\ (yy is less than or equal to 10)

  • 2a+1≠72a + 1 \neq 7\ (2a+12a + 1\ is not equal to 7)

Working with Expressions and Equations

Evaluate

To evaluate an expression means to substitute given numbers for the variables (plug them in) and then calculate the result using the order of operations (BEDMAS).

Example:

Evaluate

2x+52x + 5

when 𝑥=3:

2(3)+5=6+5=112(3) + 5 = 6 + 5 = 11

💡Learning Insight

BEDMAS is an acronym used in mathematics to remember the order of operations. It provides a set of rules that dictate the sequence in which calculations must be performed to ensure a consistent and correct result for any given mathematical expression.

The operations are performed in the following order:

  • Brackets / Parentheses

  • Exponents / Orders (or Indices)

  • Division and Multiplication (equal priority, left to right)

  • Addition and Subtraction (equal priority, left to right)

Patterns and Sequences

Patterns

A pattern is a list of numbers, shapes, or objects that follow a rule.

A pattern usually has three parts:

  • Term number — which place it is in the pattern (1st, 2nd, 3rd, …).

  • Term value — the actual number (or shape) at that place.

  • Rule / expression — the math sentence that tells how to get the term value from the term number.

Examples:

  • Pattern: 2,4,6,8,…

  • Term number: 1,2,3,4,…

  • Term value: 2,4,6,8,…

  • Rule (expression):

an=2na_{n} = 2n where 𝑛 is the term number.

If we know the rule, we can find any term in the pattern — even the 100th one — without listing all the numbers.

Sequence

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers that follows a specific rule or pattern. The numbers in the sequence are called terms.

  • Arithmetic sequence: add the same number each time.

A sequence where the difference between consecutive terms is constant. This constant difference is called the common difference.

Examples:

  • 3,5,7,9,11,… (common difference of +2)

  • 10,7,4,1,… (common difference of −3)

  • Geometric sequence: multiply by the same number each time.

A sequence where the ratio between consecutive terms is constant. This constant ratio is called the common ratio.

Example:

2,4,8,16,32,… (common ratio of ×2\times 2)
